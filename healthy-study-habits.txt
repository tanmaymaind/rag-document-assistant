Healthy Study Habits

Regular sleep is one of the strongest predictors of learning. Adults generally need seven to nine hours of sleep per night, and studying late into the night before an exam is less effective than reviewing material across several shorter sessions.

Spaced repetition means revisiting material at increasing intervals, for example after one day, after three days, and after a week. This strengthens long-term memory far more than reading the same notes repeatedly in one sitting.

Short breaks help concentration. A common pattern is to study for 25 to 50 minutes, then take a five to ten minute break away from the screen, stretch, and drink water.

Physical activity also supports focus. Even a twenty minute walk can improve mood and attention, and regular exercise is linked to better memory and lower stress during exam periods.
